The Problem

Not all water is equal. In South Africa, that matters more than most people realise.

We're told to drink more water. What we're not told is that where your water comes from and what's in it matters just as much as how much you drink. Think of it like food — a fast-food burger and an organic, grass-fed burger might both be called "beef," but your body knows the difference. Water is no different.

The Hydration Reality

Four truths about everyday water.

Spring water feels natural. Fresh. Untouched. But natural doesn't always mean clean. It reflects whatever is happening in the surrounding environment — geology, rainfall, farming activity, mining or nearby development.

Mineral content can change over time, and trace contaminants can make their way in unnoticed. Two bottles from the same spring, months apart, can be very different waters. Nature doesn't standardise. Your body prefers consistency.

Municipal water in South Africa is treated at source, but then travels through decades-old infrastructure before it reaches your glass. Along the way it can pick up unwanted extras from aging pipes, pressure fluctuations, corrosion, or local system failures.

Safe doesn't always mean optimal. And drinkable isn't the same as ideal.

Agriculture, mining, rural and urban development have changed what ends up in our water systems. Run-off from farming, trace chemicals, and environmental pollutants are increasingly detected in surface and groundwater sources — even when they're legally within limits.

They're not always dramatic. They're just not what your body asked for.

On the other side of the spectrum are ultra-purified waters: distilled, reverse-osmosis, or heavily filtered. They're clean. But they're also empty — no minerals, no electrolytes, no natural balance. It's like eating perfectly sterile food with no nourishment.

So here's the gap

You shouldn't have to choose between clean and mineral-rich.

Spring water

Natural minerals — but inconsistent, dependent on whatever the environment around the spring is doing.

Purified water

Clean and controlled — but empty, stripped of the minerals your body actually needs to hydrate well.

We realised something obvious and surprisingly missing in South Africa. You shouldn't have to choose between water that's natural and water that's clean. That's the problem we set out to solve.
